    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in New Rochelle, NY

    Commercial Wall Waterproofing in New Rochelle

    Restaurant kitchens, gym locker rooms and salon wash stations put water on walls all day. We waterproof commercial showers, mop sink walls and restroom wet walls with membranes listed for continuous wet use, and we repair the supply and drain piping behind them when we find a leak during demo. After-hours scheduling is available.

    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in New Rochelle

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. A horizontal crack often signals soil pressure pushing the wall in, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Most vertical cracks get sealed in one visit.

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in New Rochelle, NY

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and tie it into a sump pit so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. It just stops puddling on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and works where a driveway or deck blocks the wall.

    Commercial Walls Waterproofing in New Rochelle

    Commercial basements, elevator pits and mechanical rooms in New Rochelle take on water the same way houses do, just with more at stake. A wet electrical room can shut a business down. We schedule around operating hours, work with property managers on access, and follow the waterproofing specs written into the building plans for below-grade walls. Downtime stays short.

    Plumbing Leak Behind Walls in New Rochelle

    A drip from a shower valve cartridge, a sweated copper stub or a loose drain slip nut can soak a wall cavity for months before paint bubbles. We find it with a moisture meter, a thermal camera that picks up the cooler wet area, and a pressure test on the supply lines. Then we cut a clean access hole. We replace the leaking fitting or pipe section. Wet insulation comes out, and fans dry the cavity before we close it up.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in New Rochelle, NY

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head aimed at the house instead of the bed, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. Then we clean the wall, let it dry and apply a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating.

    Drainage System Integration in New Rochelle, NY

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Footing drains, interior drain tile and French drains in the yard all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a downspout emptying right at the foundation. We extend downspouts at least four feet from the wall.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in New Rochelle, NY

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized to the water coming in, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. A missing check valve lets the discharge line drain back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed at least 10 feet from the house.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in New Rochelle

    Crack injection runs about $300 to $800 per crack. A full exterior dig and membrane on a basement usually lands between $8,000 and $20,000, depending on how deep the wall is, how long it runs and what is built against it, while interior drain tile with a sump typically costs $4,000 to $12,000. Access changes the price a lot. We itemize every quote after the inspection.

    Check the basement after a heavy rain. Wet spots on the walls, mold or mildew, a musty smell in the crawl space, efflorescence on concrete, new cracks in the foundation or standing water on the floor all mean water is getting in somewhere. Those signs rarely go away on their own.

    Some handy homeowners do. Rolling on a liquid membrane is not hard, but most leaks start at the seams, inside corners and drain, and a pan also needs a correct quarter-inch-per-foot slope plus a 24-hour flood test. A pro install lowers the odds of hidden damage.

    Damp proofing is usually a thin spray-on asphalt coat. It slows soil moisture but will not stop water that is pushing against the wall under pressure. Waterproofing uses thicker membranes, drainage boards and footing drains rated to stop liquid water, which is what a basement in wet soil or a high water table needs.

    Patching one small crack yourself is fine. A full system involves excavation, digging equipment, proper membrane thickness and knowing how water drains around a footing, plus local code on drainage and backfill. A bad install can trap water against the wall and make things worse.

    Waterproofing takes away the moisture mold needs to grow. Existing mold should be removed and the area dried before we seal anything, and for larger growth, roughly anything over ten square feet, we recommend a remediation company. We remove smaller affected areas ourselves.

    Done right with good materials, a waterproofing system lasts 20 to 50 years. The range is wide since soil movement, groundwater levels and how well it was installed all play a part, and a crack that opens later can let water around even a good membrane. Drain tile can silt up over time.

    Crack repairs take a few hours. Interior drain tile usually takes one to three days, and a full exterior dig around the whole house can run three to seven days or more depending on the weather and the depth of the footing. We give you a timeline at the first visit.
