    What Goes Into an Ejector System in New Rochelle, NY

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in New Rochelle

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps seem similar at first but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. They are not interchangeable.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in New Rochelle, NY

    The basin holds everything, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. A larger basin means longer, less frequent cycles that spare the motor and switch, so we go bigger when the pit takes a laundry or several fixtures. Every lid gets sealed.

    Ejector Pump Replacement in New Rochelle

    Ejector pumps usually last 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they cycle and what gets flushed. Signs of trouble include a pump that runs constantly, a grinding or humming noise, an alarm that keeps tripping and sewage odor near the pit, and any of those means it is time to call before the basement floor tells you. The float usually goes first.

    Zoeller Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in New Rochelle

    Zoeller's sewage line covers home and heavier-duty use, in cast iron with tethered or vertical float switches. We pick the model by horsepower and pump curve for your lift, and a typical basement bathroom lands on a four-tenths or half horsepower unit with two-inch solids handling. Tall lifts need bigger pumps.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in New Rochelle

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so every fixture drains to a sealed pit instead of flowing by gravity. We saw the floor, trench drains to the basin, set it, carry a two-inch vent to the roof and run discharge to the main drain through a check valve and shutoff. Then we patch the concrete.

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in New Rochelle, NY

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and busy homes may need two-pump setups with an alternating control panel. Alternating the lead pump spreads wear, and when one fails or flow spikes, the second starts on its own and the panel sounds an alarm. Redundancy matters here.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in New Rochelle

    Digging the pit means cutting the slab and excavating below it, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Frost reaches deep into the ground here, so water service lines must be buried well below the surface to avoid freeze damage. We bed the basin on gravel and backfill around it so it cannot float when groundwater rises, then pour new concrete to seal the floor and lock the basin in place. Floating basins crack floors.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in New Rochelle

    Residential ejector systems in New Rochelle serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. The setup is a sealed basin, a pump, a float switch, a check valve and a vent. The parts are simple. Most problems come from the install, not the hardware, so we take our time on the vent and the lid seal.
