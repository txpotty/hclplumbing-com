    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Poughkeepsie

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. A large interceptor with a hardened grease cap can run past $1,000. Disposal is billed by the gallon at the receiving facility, so the gallons hauled drive most of the cost, and a 1,500-gallon tank fills a truck.

    Not if we schedule it right. We book most visits outside service hours. Indoor jobs tie up the dish area for 30 to 60 minutes, and we plan it so we are gone before prep starts.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Generally a trap is the small unit indoors, often under 100 gallons. An interceptor is the big in-ground tank outside, often 500 gallons or more, and plumbing codes treat them as separate categories with different sizing rules.

    Everything in the trap comes out, from the floating grease cap down to the sludge of food solids on the floor. Haulers call it brown grease. It is different from the yellow grease in your used fryer oil bin, which is recycled separately and should never be poured into the trap.

    Rules come from your sewer district or city pretreatment program. The 25 percent rule shows up in most of them. Many also set a maximum interval, require records on site for a year or more, and ban enzyme additives as a substitute for pumping, so we check the program that covers your address before setting a schedule.

    Skimming off the top layer yourself is usually allowed. The disposal is the part that gets people fined. Many programs want a licensed hauler's record for every cleaning, and a kitchen doing its own has nothing to show the inspector.

    Slow sinks come first. Gurgling floor drains and a sour smell by the dish station usually follow. By the time gray water is coming up through a floor sink, the trap is usually well past 25 percent full and grease is already heading down the line.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. Automatic grease removal units are on the list too. Those AGRUs have a heater and a skimmer wheel that pull grease into a container, and they need the container emptied and the wheel and screen cleaned on a schedule.
