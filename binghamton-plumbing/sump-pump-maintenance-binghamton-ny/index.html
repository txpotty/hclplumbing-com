    Float Switch Inspection in Binghamton, NY

    The float switch tells the pump when to start and stop, and a float with too little travel between on and off makes the pump short cycle dozens of times an hour. Our plumber watches the float rise and drop through a complete cycle. Next we adjust the on and off levels so each run is longer and the starts are fewer.

    Crawlspace and Window Well Pumps in Binghamton, NY

    Not every sump sits in a basement. Plenty of crawlspaces and deep window wells use a small pump to clear storm runoff. Our plumber clears the screen, tests the float and traces the discharge to daylight or an approved drain, since a line that ends under the house only relocates the water. Lake-effect snow and deep cold snaps can keep temperatures below freezing for days, so insulating exposed lines is worth it.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Service in Binghamton, NY

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, so the wear shows up in the shaft, the bearings and the float rod. We check the rod for bends and the stops for slipping. Bearings get a listen. A motor that whines or rattles on startup is usually close to done.

    Annual Sump Pump Check-Ups in Binghamton

    Once a year is the minimum. We check the motor, impeller, float and discharge line on every annual visit. After that, we fill the basin and time the drawdown, since a slow pump-out is the first hint of a tired impeller or a restricted line.

    Submersible Sump Pump Maintenance in Binghamton

    Submersible pumps sit at the bottom of the pit, which keeps them quiet but hides the problems. Our plumber pulls the unit out so we can see the cord and the shaft seal. A cracked cord usually means a new pump. The intake screen gets cleaned, and we look over the housing for corrosion, since pumps sitting in standing water for years rust from the bottom up.

    Battery Backup System Testing in Binghamton, NY

    Storms knock out power when the pit fills fastest. That is the moment a backup pump earns its keep. We check the battery date, clean the terminals and confirm the charger is actually charging, since a dead charger quietly drains the battery to nothing over a few months.

    Float Switch Repair and Adjustment in Binghamton, NY

    Float trouble causes more no-start calls than anything else. Tethered floats can wrap around the discharge pipe or snag on the pit wall. Vertical floats hang up once minerals coat the rod. For narrow basins where a float keeps catching, an electronic switch with no moving float is a good swap that ends the problem.

    You can check that the pump is plugged in and the discharge outlet outside is not buried or blocked. A bucket of water shows whether it starts. What a homeowner check misses is amp draw, check valve wear and early float problems, which take a meter and pulling the pump to see.
