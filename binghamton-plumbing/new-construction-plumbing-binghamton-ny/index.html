    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Binghamton

    Bathroom count drives the cost more than square footage does. A slab home is often a little cheaper to plumb than a basement home, because basement drains may need an ejector pump and the layout uses more vertical pipe. Gas piping is priced separately. A square-foot guess misses too much, so we bid from plans.

    Slow drains in a brand-new house are the first sign. Gurgling or a sewer smell usually means a vent or slope issue. A camera inspection through the cleanout will show a belly, an offset joint or debris left in the line, and it is worth doing before the builder's warranty period ends. Construction debris is common.

    We install Moen, Kohler, Delta and American Standard, plus most other major brands a builder or designer specifies. Homeowner-supplied fixtures are fine. We ask that they arrive on site before trim day so we can open the boxes and check for missing parts, because a missing cartridge or wrong finish can push trim back a week.

    Rough-in on a typical house takes one to two weeks of crew time, spread over the build. Underground runs a day or two before the pour. Top-out happens after framing and takes three to five days, then trim waits until cabinets and counters are in, which can be months later on a custom home. Trim is one or two days.

    We can run a dedicated return line during rough-in. Hot water then reaches distant bathrooms in seconds. A dedicated line works better than a crossover valve under a sink, which sends hot water back through the cold line and can leave the cold tap lukewarm for a moment. The pump needs an outlet.

    Scheduling causes most of the trouble. A late pour or a framing change can stack plumbing phases on top of each other. Late plan changes are next, since moving a tub after framing means more drilling and another inspection. We price changes in writing first.

    We handle the plumbing permit work and inspections on our scope. The general contractor normally holds the main permit. We book every plumbing inspection, meet the inspector, and clear corrections before the next trade needs the wall.

    Expect plumbing to touch the schedule three times. Underground takes one to three days. Top-out usually takes three to five days on a single-family house, and trim takes one or two days once cabinets, counters and tile are in place. Inspections add a day each.
