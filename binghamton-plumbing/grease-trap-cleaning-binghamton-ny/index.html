    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Binghamton

    Most visits in Binghamton run somewhere between $200 and $800, set mostly by trap size and how long it has gone since the last pump-out. Hard access adds time. Long hose runs, like 80 feet to reach a trap behind a walk-in, add to the price. You get the price first.

    Slow sinks come first. Gurgling floor drains and a sour smell by the dish station usually follow. Gray water rising through a floor sink means the trap is far past 25 percent and grease is already moving downstream.

    Watch the floor sinks during dish rush. Standing water in a floor sink is the first hint. Other signs include a rancid smell near the dish station, grease showing up in the cleanout nearest the street, and drains that gurgle after the dish machine empties.

    Grease passes the outlet and plugs the line. Then the backups start, and they tend to hit during the rush. On top of the cleanup, sewer districts can fine you and bill you for jetting the main, and sewage on the floor can get a kitchen closed by the health inspector.

    Staff can clean a small under-sink trap between pump-outs. Disposal still has to be legal. Many kitchens have staff skim daily and book us to fully pump and scrape every few weeks, which keeps the interval long without skipping the records the inspector expects.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Most people use trap for the small indoor unit, usually under 100 gallons. Interceptor usually means the large tank in the ground outside, often 500 gallons and up, and codes size the two in different ways.

    No permit is needed just to have a trap pumped. The records are your responsibility. A new or resized trap is different, usually needing a plumbing permit and sometimes a review by the pretreatment program first.

    Everything in the trap comes out, from the floating grease cap down to the sludge of food solids on the floor. Haulers call it brown grease. Yellow grease, the used fryer oil in your collection bin, is a separate product that gets recycled and should never go into the trap.
