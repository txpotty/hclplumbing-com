    Preventative Grease Trap Maintenance & Repair in Niagara Falls, NY

    Most trap repairs we do trace back to pumping intervals that stretched too long. Pretreatment programs commonly set the limit at 25 percent of the liquid depth for grease and solids combined. After a repair, we measure the grease cap and solids with a sludge judge and suggest a pumping schedule based on how fast your trap actually fills. A kitchen running two fryers can hit that mark in a couple of weeks.

    Hydro-Jetting for Grease Trap Repair in Niagara Falls

    A cable machine punches a hole through grease. Jetting actually scours the pipe wall clean. We run a commercial jetter at 3,000 to 4,000 PSI through the line feeding the trap and the line leaving it, working from the cleanout toward the main. With old cast iron, we turn the pressure down and keep a camera on it, since a thin wall can blow out.

    Flow Control Device Repair in Niagara Falls

    Hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, like 20 or 50 gallons per minute, and the flow control fitting is what holds the water to that rate. Take it out and a three-compartment sink dump blows grease right past the baffles. Inspectors look for it. We also clear the air intake, since a plugged intake on the flow control fitting lets the trap siphon.

    Residential Grease Trap Repair in Niagara Falls, NY

    Homes rarely need a grease trap, but some have one, usually a small plastic unit under the kitchen sink on a house with a septic system or a home bakery.     Grease Trap Leak Repair in Niagara Falls

    Plastic and fiberglass trap bodies do not rust, but they can crack from a dropped lid or a dishwasher dumping water hotter than the trap is rated for. Hairline cracks sometimes take a plastic weld. Anything through the floor of the tank or along a molded seam we treat as a replacement, because patches there fail under the weight of a full trap. We will show you the crack before recommending anything.

    Grease Trap Odor Control & Repair in Niagara Falls

    A sewer smell near the trap means gas is getting out somewhere it should not. The usual suspects are a torn lid gasket or a floor drain trap that has dried out. We check venting on the fixture side too, because a sink with no vent can siphon its own trap seal and let sewer gas back into the kitchen. Cleaning alone will not fix a bad gasket.

    Leaking Grease Trap Repair in Niagara Falls, NY

    A wet floor around an under-sink trap usually points to a failed lid gasket or a loose inlet coupling. Older steel bodies rust out along the bottom seams, too. We dry the area, fill the trap and watch where the water shows up. Some days the fix is a cheap gasket and ten minutes. If the seam itself has opened, patching rarely holds and we will tell you so before you pay for it.
