    Residential Grease Trap Cleaning in Syracuse, NY

    Homes rarely have a grease trap, though a few large custom kitchens and some home-based catering setups in Syracuse, NY put a small unit under the sink. Most are 10 to 20 gallon boxes. Service means scooping out grease and solids by hand, cleaning the baffle, and seating the gasket so the lid seals tight. About thirty minutes.

    Odor Control and Trap Inspections in Syracuse

    A trap that stinks between pump-outs usually has a flattened lid gasket or a vent problem. We check both on every visit. A gasket costs little to replace, while a missing or broken vent can let sewer gas into the kitchen and also slow the trap's drainage, so that one is worth tracking down. Odors are often fixed the same day.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance in Syracuse

    Pumping frequency comes from two numbers: how big the trap is and how fast your kitchen fills it. We measure the second one with a sludge judge on the first few visits. After that the date goes on our calendar and the truck arrives without a reminder call from you. Schedules shift if your menu does.

    Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in Syracuse, NY

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. Busy kitchens need them cleaned every week or two. The service covers grease and solids removal, a baffle scrape, a look at the inlet flow control fitting, and a new lid gasket if the old one is flattened. A bad gasket is why most of these smell.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Cleaning in Syracuse

    Gravity interceptors in the parking lot often hold 750 to 2,000 gallons across two or three compartments. We pump each compartment on its own. A hard grease cap gets broken up with a pressure wand before pumping, otherwise it plugs the suction hose and half of it stays in the tank. No one goes inside without confined-space gear.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance Plans in Syracuse

    Most pretreatment programs use the 25 percent rule: pump before floating grease and settled solids together take up a quarter of the trap's liquid depth. Some cities set a fixed interval instead, often 90 days for interceptors. We work to whichever rule applies. Changes are easy.

    Commercial Grease Trap Cleaning in Syracuse

    Restaurants and hospital kitchens in Syracuse run the same basic setup, a trap or interceptor sitting between the sinks and the sewer, sized by flow rate or by gallons. The crew pumps it out and scrapes it down. We check both tees and the baffle wall for cracks or missing parts before closing it up. Intervals run anywhere from two weeks to three months.

    Outdoor Grease Interceptor Pumping in Syracuse, NY

    In-ground interceptors need a vacuum truck and usually a pressure wand to break up the cap. We open every compartment and pump each one to the floor. With the tank empty, we inspect the tees, baffle wall, risers and lid seals, since a cracked riser lets dirt and groundwater into the tank. Lids go back on tight.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Syracuse

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. Large outdoor interceptors can pass $1,000 when they are full and the cap has hardened. The biggest variable is the gallons hauled, because disposal facilities charge by volume and a 1,500-gallon tank is a full truckload.

    Skimming off the top layer yourself is usually allowed. Disposing of it is where people get in trouble. Many programs want a licensed hauler's record for every cleaning, and a kitchen doing its own has nothing to show the inspector.

    It depends on trap size and menu. Commercial kitchens usually land between every two weeks and every three months. Homes with a small unit may go a year, but a fryer-heavy kitchen on a 50-gallon trap might need pumping every other week.

    It goes to a permitted receiving facility. Some of it ends up as biofuel or digester feedstock. The rest is dewatered and disposed of under the facility's permit, and every load we haul is logged so the trail runs from your trap to the gate.

    Two things, a full pump-out and a trap that drains properly when we leave. Skim-and-go is not how we work. If a drain problem traced to our visit shows up afterward, we send the crew back to fix it, and the depth readings on the sheet show what the trap looked like when we finished.

    Fryer volume drives the schedule more than seat count. Plenty of busy restaurants pump monthly. Lighter kitchens, like a coffee shop with a small food menu, can stretch to every two or three months as long as depth readings stay under the 25 percent line.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Generally a trap is the small unit indoors, often under 100 gallons. An interceptor is the big in-ground tank outside, often 500 gallons or more, and plumbing codes treat them as separate categories with different sizing rules.

    FOG stands for fats, oils and grease. It pours as a liquid and sets up hard once it cools. In a sewer pipe it sticks to the walls in layers, catches food scraps and wipes, and eventually closes the pipe down to a pencil-sized opening.
