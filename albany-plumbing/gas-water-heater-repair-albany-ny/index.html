    Propane Water Heater Repair in Albany

    Rural properties often run water heaters on propane rather than natural gas. Propane units use different orifices and regulator pressures, and incorrect conversions cause sooting and poor performance. We confirm the correct parts are installed and repair pilots, valves and burners. We also check tank regulators for correct pressure.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Albany

    Power vent heaters use a blower to push exhaust out through PVC pipe. Failures usually involve the blower motor, pressure switch, hot surface igniter or condensate in the vent. We read the error codes, test each part and repair the failure. Correct vent slope and termination are checked before we leave.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Troubleshooting in Albany

    Odd noises coming from your gas water heater, such as rumbling, popping or whistling, often indicate sediment buildup in the tank or a burner problem. Hard water can add to sediment accumulation. Our repair service includes flushing the tank and inspecting for sediment, addressing pressure issues or checking other components that might be causing the noise, restoring efficiency and quiet operation.

    Tank Leaks and Pressure Problems in Albany

    Even gas water heaters can develop tank leaks, often due to corrosion from hard water or high water pressure. Water hardness in New York varies a lot, with many surface water supplies running soft and groundwater in some areas running hard. While some leaks may call for a replacement, minor leaks or pressure relief valve issues can often be repaired. Our team assesses the extent of the damage, inspects the anode rod and addresses pressure imbalances to prevent further complications in your Albany home.

    Many Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves use flash codes to report problems like a weak thermopile or a tripped sensor. We read the code and test the related part.

    A thermocouple is a safety device in a gas water heater that senses if the pilot light is lit. If it doesn't sense heat, it shuts off the gas supply to prevent gas leaks. A faulty thermocouple is a leading reason for a pilot light not staying lit and is a critical safety component.

    While some minor issues might seem DIY friendly, working with gas appliances carries significant safety risks, including gas leaks and carbon monoxide poisoning.
