    What a Toilet Replacement Covers in Brentwood, NY

    Standard Toilet Replacement in Brentwood

    Two-piece toilets are what most houses have, and after twenty or thirty years the tank bolts corrode, the porcelain gets crazed and the flush never quite clears the bowl. That is when we swap them. We pull the old toilet, scrape the flange and check it for cracks, then set the new one on a fresh wax ring with new closet bolts.

    Toilet Rough-In Adjustments in Brentwood, NY

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the drain, usually 12 inches, sometimes 10 or 14 in older homes. Take the measurement from the wall to the closet bolts, not from the baseboard trim. If the new toilet's rough-in does not match, it either sits too far out or will not fit. An offset flange can correct an inch or two without moving the drain.

    ADA and Commercial Toilet Replacement in Brentwood, NY

    Public restrooms need bowls rated for heavy use, and many need to meet ADA height and clearance rules when a fixture is replaced. Clearances get checked before we order anything. Sensor flush valves are an easy add during the swap, and they cut down on unflushed bowls.

    Commercial Flush Valve Upgrades in Brentwood

    Manual flush valves on older commercial toilets leak at the handle and stick open, and swapping to a sensor valve during a replacement improves hygiene without changing the rough-in. You can pick battery or hardwired sensors. We do the work after hours if needed, and we check that the new fixtures meet accessibility requirements.

    One-Piece Toilet Replacement in Brentwood, NY

    One-piece toilets have no tank-to-bowl joint, so there are fewer leak points and fewer crevices to clean. They are heavier and pricier than two-piece models. A one-piece toilet usually takes two people to carry upstairs, and we set it without dragging it across your tile so the floor stays unscratched and the wax ring lands centered on the flange.

    Urgent Toilet Replacement in Brentwood, NY

    Late-night failures happen, usually a tank that splits along a seam or a bowl that cracks at the base and keeps seeping no matter how many towels go down. Closing the angle stop stops the water. We can usually replace it the next morning, and we will tell you if the floor needs to dry before we set the new one.

    Choosing a Toilet Brand in Brentwood, NY
