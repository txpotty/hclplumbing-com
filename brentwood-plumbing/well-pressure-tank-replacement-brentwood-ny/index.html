    Pressure Switch and Gauge Integration in Brentwood, NY

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Switch and tank are a team. A switch set at 40/60 needs a tank pre-charged to 38 psi, and a gauge that reads correctly so you can see what the system is doing, so we replace a worn gauge rather than trusting a sticky needle. Small parts, big role.

    Emergency Pressure Tank Replacement in Brentwood, NY

    If the pump is running nonstop or the taps are dry, call (716) 396-7101. A ruptured tank or a stuck switch can burn out a pump, so we isolate the system, diagnose it and swap the tank, often the same day. Turn off the breaker first.

    Preventative Well Pressure Tank Maintenance in Brentwood, NY

    Check the air charge once a year. Shut off the pump, drain the system to zero, and put a tire gauge on the valve at the top of the tank, then add air with a small compressor if it has dropped. Water out of the valve means a torn bladder.

    Residential Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Brentwood

    Most homes on a well have a tank between 20 and 86 gallons sitting near the pressure switch. We shut off power to the pump first. Then we drain the system, unthread the old tank from the tank tee, set the pre-charge on the new one, and connect it with new fittings and fresh sealant. Usually a morning.

    Galvanized Steel Pressure Tank Upgrades in Brentwood

    Old galvanized tanks have no bladder at all. Water sits right against an air pocket. Over time the water absorbs the air, the tank waterlogs, and someone has to drain and recharge it every few months. A sealed bladder tank ends that chore.

    Emergency Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Brentwood

    A tank that bursts or a pump that runs nonstop needs attention fast. Turn the pump breaker off right away. A pump that keeps running against a failed system can overheat and burn out, so we isolate the problem and get a new tank in, often the same day. Water back tonight.

    Well-Rite Pressure Tank Installation in Brentwood, NY

    Pressure Tank Sizing and Upsizing in Brentwood

    Drawdown is the usable water between cut-out and cut-in. Small tanks give surprisingly little. We divide pump flow by drawdown to see how many starts per hour the pump will face, aiming for at least one minute of run time per cycle. That often points to a bigger tank.
