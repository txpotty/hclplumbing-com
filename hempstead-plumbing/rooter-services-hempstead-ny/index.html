    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Hempstead

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet usually costs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. Adding a jetting pass brings the total to about $450 to $1,000, depending on line length and how much grease is in it. After-hours calls cost more.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Hempstead's established homes take the most damage. They also put our techs at risk. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.

    Our trucks carry Spartan and RIDGID machines, plus jetters sized for residential and commercial lines. They hold up well to heavy daily use. Several sizes of cable and cutter ride on each truck, so the tech matches the tool to the pipe rather than using one setup everywhere.

    Routine drain cleaning in Hempstead, NY does not usually need a permit. Digging up the yard, replacing the sewer or making a major repair usually requires one. We handle that paperwork. You will know about any permit before repair work is scheduled.

    Most single-drain clogs take 1 to 3 hours from arrival to flow test. Main lines with heavy roots, or jobs that need a jetting pass, can stretch to half a day or more. Access makes the biggest difference. With no usable cleanout, we pull a toilet to get in, and that adds about an hour.

    Rooting is safe with a trained tech and the correct cable and blade. Collapsed or badly cracked pipe can be hurt by anything run through it. The tech's touch matters. We stop if the pipe shows damage, and we bring in the camera to look before going any further down the line.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. A jetter scours the walls with water at high pressure. Many tough lines do best with both, cut first to open the line and jetted second to wash out what the blades left.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. A jetter uses water at thousands of psi to scour the inside of the pipe from wall to wall. Jetting leaves the pipe cleaner. We usually jet recurring grease lines and scaled mains, and snake the one-off clogs.
