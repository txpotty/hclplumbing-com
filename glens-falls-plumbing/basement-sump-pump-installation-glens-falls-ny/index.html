    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Glens Falls

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor above the pit, which makes them easier to service and a good fit for narrow pits in older homes. They usually last a long time because the motor never sits in water. The tradeoff is more noise and a less finished look, so we generally recommend them for utility rooms rather than living spaces. We install them with a rigid discharge, a quality check valve and a float rod set to your pit depth.

    Sump Pump Installation Cost Comparison in Glens Falls, NY

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit is the least expensive option, typically a few hundred dollars plus the pump. Digging a new pit through the slab adds labor for saw cutting, excavation and concrete patching. Adding a battery backup raises the total but often costs less than one cleanup after a flooded basement. We give you an itemized quote so you can compare a basic primary pump against a full primary and backup system before we begin.

    Commercial and Multi-Unit Sump Installations in Glens Falls

    Office buildings, parking structures and multi-unit properties with below-grade levels often need duplex sump systems with two pumps and an alternating control panel. The panel switches lead and lag pumps on each cycle so wear stays even, and a high water alarm alerts building staff. We coordinate electrical with your electrician and follow manufacturer specs for basin size and float settings. Property managers get a clear maintenance schedule to keep both pumps exercised.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Glens Falls

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by locating the low point where water collects. We saw cut the slab, excavate a pit roughly 18 to 24 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a perforated or solid liner depending on whether we are collecting groundwater or surface seepage. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor stays clean and level.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Glens Falls

    The cost for basement sump pump installation in Glens Falls, NY typically ranges from $800 to $2,500, depending on the type of pump, pedestal or submersible, whether a new pit needs to be excavated and whether a battery backup is included. We provide itemized estimates after an on-site assessment.

    We routinely install Zoeller, Liberty Pumps and Wayne because they offer cast iron housings, reliable switches and good parts support. We will fit the brand and model to your pit size and budget.

    A standard basement sump pump installation typically takes 4 to 8 hours. If a new sump pit needs to be excavated, or there are unforeseen challenges with plumbing or electrical access, the job might stretch to a full day. Our team works efficiently to limit disruption to your home.

    Most residential basements need a 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump, but the right answer depends on how fast water enters the pit and how high it must be lifted. We measure both before recommending a model.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, built to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that kicks on if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for the best protection.

    Spring snowmelt and heavy rain can raise water tables quickly in New York, sending water into basements and testing sump pumps. Because storms and power outages tend to arrive together, a battery backup sump pump is strongly recommended. It offers crucial protection by switching on automatically when the main power fails, so your basement stays dry even without electricity. It is a wise investment for peace of mind.

    Lead service lines remain in some older neighborhoods, and replacing them is a frequent recommendation during major plumbing work. Homes built on slabs without basements seldom need one. Homes that do have basements or below-grade rooms can take on water quickly during heavy storms, especially where the soil sheds rain instead of absorbing it.

    A submersible sump pump sits entirely within the sump pit, making it quieter and less obtrusive. A pedestal sump pump has its motor above the pit, making it easier to access for maintenance and usually more affordable. Both types are effective for removing water from your basement.
