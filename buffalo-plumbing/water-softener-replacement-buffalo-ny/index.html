    Replacing Fleck Water Softeners in Buffalo, NY

    Fleck control valves, the 5600SXT and 9100SXT in particular, are on a large share of the softeners we see in New York homes. If the valve is old enough that parts are hard to find, a new valve on a new tank is the smarter buy. Newer Fleck heads drop right in.

    Dual Tank Softener Systems in Buffalo

    Twin-tank systems keep one tank in service while the other regenerates, so you never get hard water during a middle-of-the-night regeneration. Kinetico's version uses water pressure, not power, to switch tanks. The price is higher.

    Clack Water Softener Replacement in Buffalo

    Clack WS1 valves are easy to program and easy to rebuild, which is why a lot of water treatment shops have switched to them. We can replace just the valve on a good tank. Sometimes the whole unit goes.

    Urgent Softener Problems in Buffalo

    Not every softener failure is urgent. A unit leaking from the valve body or the brine tank overflowing onto the floor is, and we move those calls up the schedule. Turn the bypass handle and wait for us.

    Fleck Valve Programming in Buffalo, NY

    Programming is where most Fleck replacements go wrong. A Fleck needs hardness, household size and regen time entered right, otherwise it cycles too often or runs out of capacity. We set it before we leave.

    Commercial Softener Replacement in Buffalo

    Espresso machines and commercial dishwashers scale up fast on hard water, and the service bills for descaling them are steep. We size commercial softeners to flow rate and daily gallons. Sometimes a carbon filter goes ahead of it.

    Brine Tank Replacement in Buffalo

    Salt bridges, crusty layers of salt that form over a hollow, stop the brine from making contact with the water and the softener quits softening. We break them up and clean out the mush at the bottom. Cracked tanks and stuck safety floats get replaced. Pellets bridge less than rock salt.

    Softener Loop Installation in Buffalo, NY

    Softener loops are the pair of stubbed-out lines near the garage wall or water heater where a softener ties into the main. Homes without one get a loop cut in, with shutoffs and a bypass. Hose bibs stay off it. We run the drain to a standpipe or floor drain through an air gap.
