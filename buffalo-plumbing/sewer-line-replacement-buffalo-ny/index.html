    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Replacement in Buffalo, NY

    Orangeburg is tar-soaked wood fiber. Many mid-century houses got it, and by now most of it has blistered and flattened enough to snag waste. Clay is tougher but leaks roots. When the camera shows trouble in three or more places, a full replacement is usually cheaper than chasing spot repairs.

    Residential Sewer Replacement in Buffalo, NY

    Most of our sewer work is single-family homes. Much of New York sits on glacial till, rock and clay, which makes excavation for sewer and water lines slower and harder. The usual suspects are rooted clay joints, cast iron worn through at the bottom and sections that sank as the soil settled. Replacing the line stops sewage from backing up into the lowest drain in the house.

    What Sewer Replacement Costs in Buffalo, NY

    Four things set the price: length, depth, access and method. Trenching an open yard tends to be cheapest per foot, and bursting costs more per foot but avoids tearing out concrete.     Upgrading Old Sewer Pipe Materials in Buffalo

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all fail in their own ways. Roots get into clay joints, cast iron rusts out along the invert, and Orangeburg fiber pipe squashes flat over the years. Modern plastic pipe avoids all three problems. HDPE is fused into one continuous piece with no joints for roots to find.

    Pipe Bursting Under Driveways in Buffalo, NY

    Concrete is expensive to replace. Bursting lets us run new HDPE under a driveway, patio or mature tree by pulling it through the path of the old pipe from a launch pit to a receiving pit. The HDPE is butt-fused on site into one long piece. Most bursts finish in a day. You end up patching two small pits rather than a whole slab.
