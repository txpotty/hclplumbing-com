    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Buffalo

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps seem similar at first but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.

    Zoeller Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Buffalo

    Zoeller's sewage line covers home and heavier-duty use, in cast iron with tethered or vertical float switches. We pick the model by horsepower and pump curve for your lift, and a typical basement bathroom lands on a four-tenths or half horsepower unit with two-inch solids handling. Tall lifts need bigger pumps.

    Emergency Ejector Pump Replacement in Buffalo, NY

    A dead ejector pump takes every below-grade fixture out of service until it is fixed. Stop using those fixtures, including the washer if it drains to the pit, then call (716) 396-7101 and we will try to get a plumber and a replacement pump to you that day. Replacements are often quick.

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Buffalo

    Liberty ejector pumps are a common pick on our jobs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models.     Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Buffalo

    Macerating systems like Saniflo add a bath fixture or two without breaking the concrete floor. The unit sits behind the toilet, grinds the waste and pumps it through a small discharge line, often three-quarter inch or one inch, up to the nearest drain, which makes it a good fit for a small basement half bath. Venting still applies.
