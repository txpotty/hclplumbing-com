    How We Install Showers in Buffalo, NY

    Shower Valve and Fixture Installation in Buffalo, NY

    Pressure-balancing valves are the baseline for any new shower. Thermostatic valves step up from there, holding an exact temperature and letting you set volume separately, which suits rain heads, handhelds and body sprays. Both prevent scalding. The outlet count usually decides it.

    Shower Valve and Trim Installation in Buffalo, NY

    The valve sits behind the tile for decades. We install pressure-balancing, thermostatic and diverter valves, set to the depth your trim kit needs and plumbed with integral stops when the valve has them. Handles and the head come later. That timing keeps the trim from getting scratched or caked with thinset while the tile is set.

    Rain and Multi-Head Shower Installation in Buffalo

    Rain heads pull up to 2.5 gallons per minute each. Pair a rain head with a handheld and body sprays and a 40-gallon water heater may run cold halfway through. The water heater gets sized up. A thermostatic valve manages each outlet.

    Tiled Walk-In Shower Installation in Buffalo

    Frameless glass walk-ins are popular, and the plumbing behind them has to be dead on. Valve, head, handheld and drain go in first, then the pan is sloped a quarter inch per foot to the drain. Testing follows. The valve gets a pressure test and the pan a 24-hour flood test before tile.

    Digital Shower Systems in Buffalo, NY

    Digital shower systems like U by Moen and Kohler DTV need a little more planning. These valves usually sit in a nearby closet or vanity and need low-voltage wiring to the controller and an outlet close by. An electrician handles the outlet. Our part is the plumbing.

    Custom Shower Enclosure Plumbing in Buffalo

    Body sprays and steam units add lines and planning. Every body spray needs a supply line, and the sizing has to be right so the farthest outlet still has good pressure. Pressure varies by house. A gauge reading comes first.

    Custom Shower System Installation in Buffalo, NY

    Multi-head showers depend on valve capacity. One head works fine off a standard valve, while multiple outlets need a thermostatic valve, volume controls and larger 3/4-inch supply lines. Layout comes first. The water heater gets checked for capacity too.

    Tub-to-Shower Conversion in Buffalo, NY

    Tub-to-shower conversions are one of our most common jobs. The tub comes out, the drain shifts to the pan location and the valve moves up to about 48 inches. Slab homes need concrete work. No more stepping over a tub wall.
