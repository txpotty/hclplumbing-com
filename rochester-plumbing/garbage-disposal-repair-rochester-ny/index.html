    Noise and Odor Diagnosis in Rochester

    A rattle that stops after a few seconds is usually a spoon or a pit bouncing in the chamber. A constant high whine means something else. That whine often comes from worn motor bearings, and once bearings start to fail the noise only gets worse until the motor seizes. Odors usually trace to the splash guard. We pull it, clean the chamber walls and replace the guard if it is torn.

    Leaking Disposal Fix in Rochester, NY

    A drip at the top usually means the sink flange putty has dried out. We drop the unit, pull the flange, clean off the old putty and reset it with fresh putty and new gaskets, then hang the disposal back up. That fixes most top leaks. A leak on the side is usually the discharge gasket or a loose hose clamp.

    Leak Detection and Repair in Rochester, NY

    Disposal leaks come from four places: the sink flange, the dishwasher inlet, the discharge tube and the housing itself. We dry everything, run water with a little food coloring and watch to see which joint shows color first, since water runs down the outside of the unit and hides the real source. Three of the four are fixable. When the housing leaks, the unit has to go.

    Grinding Noise Repair in Rochester

    A metal-on-metal grind may mean a loose mount, a foreign object or worn impellers. After checking the chamber, snugging the mount and pulling debris, we run it empty to hear if the noise is coming from the motor. Failing bearings are not worth fixing.

    Jam Clearing and Motor Repair in Rochester

    Bones, fruit pits and bottle caps lodge between the grind ring and the impeller lugs, and the motor stalls. We shut off power, insert a 1/4 inch hex key into the socket on the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until the flywheel turns freely. Then the object comes out with pliers. A motor that stalled for a long time may have overheated, so we test it under load before calling the job done.

    Preventive Care and Longevity in Rochester, NY

    Run cold water before, during and after grinding, and let it go about 15 seconds after the motor stops so the food clears the trap. Grease poured in with hot water coats the chamber and the pipe, while cold water keeps it solid so the impellers can break it up. Fibrous scraps jam it most. Throw out celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.
