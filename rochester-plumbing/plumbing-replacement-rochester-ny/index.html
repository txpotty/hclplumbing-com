    Well Pump and Pressure Tank Replacement in Rochester, NY

    Rural properties near Rochester, NY depend on the well pump for every drop, so a failure is an emergency. Watch for rising and falling pressure, sputtering taps, nonstop running or a breaker that trips, since each points to the pump, switch or tank. A failing pump seldom gets better.

    Water Softener Replacement in Rochester, NY

    Softeners wear out as the resin breaks down and the control valve stops cycling properly. Homeowners around Rochester with hard water often add a softener to protect water heaters and keep mineral buildup out of faucets. Once scale returns, dishes spot or salt stops dropping, we test hardness, size a new unit and plumb it with a bypass so the house keeps water during service. Salt use often drops too.

    Whole-Home Pipe Replacement in Rochester

    Several pinhole leaks, galvanized pipe or polybutylene in a Rochester home usually mean it is better off with a complete repipe. We replace the supply lines with PEX or copper, often routing new pipe through the attic and down interior walls so a leaking line under the slab can be abandoned instead of jackhammered out.     Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Rochester, NY

    Switching to tankless when the old tank fails is common in Rochester, NY, but it is a bigger job than a tank swap. The gas draw can be four times a 40-gallon tank, so the meter line usually gets upsized, and the venting moves from B-vent to stainless or PVC. Both are on the quote.

    Pipe Replacement and Repiping in Rochester

    Corroded, leaking or galvanized pipe in Rochester leads to rusty water and expensive leaks. Failing sections or the whole house get new PEX or copper, routed to keep openings small and pressure tested long enough to catch any slow drip. Walls get patched.

    Repair vs Replacement Cost Comparison in Rochester, NY

    A common rule of thumb is to replace when a repair costs more than half the price of new, or when the part is near the end of its life. Take a 12-year-old heater with a bad gas valve, where the valve alone costs a third of a new unit and the tank is already near the end. We price both.
