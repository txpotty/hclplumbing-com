    Repipe Costs Versus Leak Repairs in New York, NY

    A whole-home PEX repipe for an average house often runs $5,000 to $12,000, with copper costing more. One slab leak with access and repair often costs $1,000 to $4,000. Do the math. By the second or third leak, a repipe usually pays for itself, and we put both options side by side so you can decide.

    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in New York

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs. We use Uponor or similar PEX-A with cold expansion fittings that open the pipe end and let it shrink back over the fitting for a full-bore joint. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. Two or three days covers most houses.

    Emergency Pipe Failures and Repipes in New York

    Sometimes a house reaches the repipe point all at once, with a burst line and two more leaks right behind it. We stop the active leak first, make temporary repairs that get water back on and then plan the full repipe so you are not rushed into it. That gives you time. Your policy may pay for water damage even if not for the new pipe, so we take photos of everything we find.

    Slab Leak Repiping in New York

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Frost reaches deep into the ground here, so water service lines must be buried well below the surface to avoid freezing. Rather than jackhammer to reach one leak, we run new lines through the attic and walls and leave the old under-slab pipe out of service. No jackhammer needed. It ends this leak and takes the rest of the slab plumbing out of the picture.

    PEX Repiping in New York, NY

    PEX is flexible plastic tubing that can run long distances without a fitting, which means fewer joints hidden in your walls. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. PEX shrugs off scale and chlorine, survives freezing better than copper and installs faster, so it is our most common repipe material. Our PEX-A installs use expansion fittings that do not shrink the flow path. That keeps pressure up at the far fixtures.

    Urgent Repipes After Repeated Failures in New York, NY

    A widespread pipe failure sometimes forces a repipe sooner than you planned. We make the house safe and usable first, with temporary repairs that get you water, then schedule the full repipe as soon as the crew and permit allow. We move fast. Even urgent repipes usually take just two to four days after we begin.

    Whole-Home Copper Repiping in New York

    Copper is the traditional choice, and Type L copper has a thicker wall than the Type M that went into many older homes. Type L tolerates UV, stays rigid and has decades of field history. It costs more. We sweat joints with lead-free solder or press them, hang the pipe at proper support intervals and test the whole system before patching.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in New York, NY

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, and over decades the inside diameter closes down to a fraction of what it was. Pressure drops slowly. Rust-colored water in the morning, poor pressure upstairs and dripping threaded joints all point to failing galvanized. We replace it with PEX or copper. Most homeowners notice the pressure difference the first time they turn on the shower.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in New York

    Most PEX repipes for New York homes cost $5,000 to $12,000, based on size, bathroom count and access. Copper typically adds 20 to 40 percent.

    We have to open some drywall, but the openings are small and planned. Openings are usually small. Each cut is a clean square at a fixture or along the route, and we patch and texture all of them so the walls are ready for paint.

    Rusty or brown water, low pressure at several fixtures and repeated pinhole leaks are the classic signs. Noisy pipes can be another. Homes with galvanized, polybutylene or Kitec should plan a repipe before a failure does it for them, because those pipes fail system-wide.

    Whole-home repipes require a permit and inspection in most New York jurisdictions. Our office handles the permit and inspections.

    Copper is rigid metal pipe with a long track record. PEX bends instead. PEX is cheaper, quicker and quieter and handles freezes better, while copper tolerates sun and heat and suits owners who want metal, so it depends on your house.

    You get a written workmanship warranty covering labor and materials. Terms are on the estimate. Our repipes use Uponor PEX-A or domestic Type L copper, and certified PEX-A jobs also get the manufacturer's long-term warranty.

    Both work well, and the best choice depends on your house and your water. PEX costs less. PEX bends, resists scale and handles freezes better, and copper is rigid, UV-tolerant and proven but pricier and vulnerable to aggressive water.

    A documented repipe removes a known risk that buyers and inspectors look for. It helps at sale time. Replacing polybutylene, Kitec or old galvanized makes a home easier to insure and easier to sell, and a permit record plus a transferable warranty gives the next owner proof the work was done.
