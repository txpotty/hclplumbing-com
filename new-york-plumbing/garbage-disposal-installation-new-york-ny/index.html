    Disposal Installation in New York, NY: The Details

    Dishwasher Connection With Disposal Installation in New York

    Every new disposal has a plug sealing the dishwasher inlet. We knock it out with a screwdriver and hammer, fish the plug out of the chamber and connect the hose with a new clamp. Forgetting the plug floods the dishwasher. We also install an air gap or high loop, so dirty sink water cannot flow back into the dishwasher while the disposal is draining.

    High-Horsepower Disposal Installation in New York

    A 3/4 or 1 HP unit is bigger and heavier than a starter model, so we measure the cabinet first. Taller units can push the discharge outlet below the wall stub, and when that happens we either lower the drain at the wall or pick a model with a higher outlet so water does not sit in the chamber. We also check the circuit. A larger motor pulls more current when it starts.

    Same-Day Disposal Installation in New York, NY

    When the old disposal dies and the sink is full of water, we can usually install a new one the same day. We carry 1/2 and 3/4 HP units, mounts and cord kits on the truck, so there is no supply house run before your sink drains. Most swaps take about an hour.

    Commercial Disposal Setup in New York

    Restaurants and commercial kitchens in New York need disposals built for constant use. They usually need their own circuit, a bigger drain and a panel that runs water on its own, and some sewer districts restrict them, so we confirm the rules first. Downtime costs money.

    Air Switch Disposal Installation in New York, NY

    Air switches start the disposal with a button on the sink or counter. Pressing the button sends a pulse of air through a small tube to a control box under the sink, so there is no electricity at the button and no need to drill for a wall switch. Islands use them often.

    Standard Disposal Installation in New York

    Most households in New York do well with a continuous-feed unit between 1/3 and 1/2 horsepower. We remove the old unit if there is one, set a new flange in fresh plumber's putty, hang the disposal on a new mount and connect it to the trap with a new discharge tube and gasket. Wiring and testing come last. A smooth spin and a clean drain tell us it is right.

    Disposal Rough-In for New Construction in New York

    Rough-in decides where the outlet and drain end up before the cabinets go in. For a kitchen with a disposal, we usually set the drain stub lower than a plain sink would need, so the trap can sit below the disposal outlet with room for proper fall. The electrician adds the switched outlet. We mark its spot so it clears the trap.

    Disposal Installation on an Existing Sink in New York

    For a common 1/2 or 3/4 HP disposal, we remove the old unit if there is one and install the new one on the existing sink. We connect it to the drain, set the P-trap and wire it, then check the clearance under the sink, which can be tight in older New York homes with shallow cabinets or a water filter mounted on the back wall. Tight cabinets need planning. Sometimes a compact model is the better fit.
