    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in New York

    The cost of grease trap cleaning in New York, NY varies based on the size of the trap, its accessibility, and the amount of accumulated FOG. Typically, prices can range from $200 for smaller under-sink units to over $1,000 for large outdoor interceptors. We provide transparent, upfront quotes after assessing your specific needs.

    Indoor traps typically take 30 minutes to an hour. Outdoor interceptors can take one to two hours depending on size and how much buildup is present.

    FOG stands for Fats, Oils, and Grease. When poured down drains, FOG cools and solidifies, sticking to pipe walls and eventually leading to severe clogs. In grease traps, it separates from wastewater, but if not regularly removed, it overflows into the sewer system, causing blockages and environmental issues.

    Slow sink drains, foul odors, grease on the water surface near the outlet and backups from floor drains are common signs. If you notice any of these, schedule a cleaning right away.

    The terms are often used interchangeably, but 'grease interceptor' typically refers to larger, outdoor units with capacities over 500 gallons, while 'grease trap' usually describes smaller, indoor units under 100 gallons, such as under-sink models. Both serve the same function of separating FOG from wastewater.

    Staff can sometimes clean very small under-sink traps, but waste must be disposed of properly. Outdoor interceptors require a vacuum truck and a licensed hauler, and many cities require professional service records.

    Yes, we provide a service record after every cleaning. It includes the date, volume removed and condition of the trap, which you can show the inspector.

    The frequency of grease trap cleaning depends heavily on usage. Commercial kitchens often require cleaning every 1 to 3 months, while residential units might only need it once a year or less. Local New York health codes for restaurants may mandate specific intervals.
