    What a Plumbing Inspection Covers in New York, NY

    Leak Detection and Assessment in New York, NY

    Hidden leaks waste water and quietly damage floors and walls. Start at the meter. We shut everything off, check the meter's leak dial and then follow the leak with acoustic equipment and thermal imaging. Catching it early keeps it small. Basements are standard in most homes, and many of them depend on sump pumps to manage groundwater during wet seasons.

    Water Heater Inspections in New York

    Water heaters give clues long before they fail. The serial number tells us the age, and we check for rust, look at the vent and test the relief valve. We check tankless units for scale. On gas units, we look at the burner and flame, check for a sediment trap and confirm the vent is drafting so combustion gases go outside.

    Rental and Commercial Property Inspections in New York

    Landlords and property managers use our inspections between tenants or before buying an investment property. We document each unit. We note heaters, fixtures and sewers by unit, and commercial jobs can include backflow and grease lines, giving owners a budget for repairs.

    Hard Water Damage Assessment in New York

    Water hardness in New York varies a lot, with many surface water supplies running soft and groundwater in some areas running hard. Hard water leaves scale in pipes, fixtures and water heaters. We look for crusty aerators, scaled showerheads, slow-filling toilets and sediment in the water heater, and we can run a simple hardness test on the spot. Then we explain your options. Softeners, filters and scale reducers all help, and we suggest the one that matches the reading we took.

    Pre-Purchase Plumbing Inspections in New York

    A house can pass a general inspection and still have a sewer full of roots. Buyers use our inspection to learn the pipe type, the sewer condition and the heater's age before the option period ends. Findings help with negotiations. Old galvanized, a damaged sewer or an aging heater can all become a repair request or a closing credit.

    Water Pressure and Supply Pipe Review in New York, NY

    We measure static water pressure and check the pressure reducing valve if one is installed. High pressure ages fixtures fast. Anything over 80 psi cuts the life of hoses, fixtures and heaters, and we note the supply pipe type, from copper and CPVC to poly, Kitec and galvanized.

    Preventive Maintenance Checks in New York, NY

    A maintenance check is a lighter version of a full inspection for owners who want to stay ahead of problems. We flush the water heater, exercise the main shutoff and angle stops, check pressure and look for slow drains and weeping fittings. It takes about an hour. Doing this every year or two adds years to a water heater and keeps the shutoffs working for the day you really need them.

    Commercial Plumbing Inspections in New York, NY

    Commercial buildings have equipment homes do not, so our commercial inspections cover more ground. We look at backflow assemblies, grease interceptors, commercial water heaters, flush valves and the building sewer. Inspectors like documentation. The written report helps facility managers plan and budget repairs before a health inspector or the water provider writes them up.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in New York

    Most home plumbing inspections run $150 to $350, depending on the size and age of the house. Sewer video is an add-on. A sewer camera adds to the total, and you hear the full price before we arrive, so the invoice matches the quote.

    High water bills, repeat clogs, slow drains and discolored water are common signs. Banging pipes count too. Knocking pipes, a rumbling heater or mold near fixtures usually mean something underneath needs a professional look.

    We inspect all the common brands and materials. Heaters from every major maker are covered. Pipe types include PEX, copper, CPVC, PVC, cast iron, galvanized and polybutylene, and we know the typical failure points for each one along with the fixture brands found in most homes.

    A one to two year routine works for most homes. Older homes or those with past leaks do well with yearly checks.

    No permit is needed for an inspection. Permits apply to the work. Permits come into play for repairs, replacements or new installations that follow from the inspection, and if any of those apply, we will tell you up front and handle the paperwork if we do the work.

    A standard home inspection takes about one and a half to three hours. Camera work takes longer. Larger homes, long sewer lines or a hidden leak that needs tracing can push it longer, and we will tell you the expected time when you book so you can plan around it.

    A general home inspector checks basic function, while a plumber goes deeper with sewer cameras, pressure tests and pipe identification. Many buyers hire both.

    Our technicians inspect all major water heater brands, including Rheem, AO Smith and Bradford White. Tankless is included. Rinnai and Noritz tankless units and most household fixture brands are routine, so your equipment brand will not be a problem.
