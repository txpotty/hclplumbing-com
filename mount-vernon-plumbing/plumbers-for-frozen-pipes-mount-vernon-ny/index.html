    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Mount Vernon

    Thawing alone usually runs a couple hundred dollars. After-hours calls cost more. The price depends on how hard the frozen section is to reach, how long it takes to thaw, and whether the line needs repair once it is flowing again, which we quote before starting that part.

    A faucet that trickles or runs dry after a cold night is the most common sign. Visible frost on a pipe confirms it. A toilet that will not refill or a bulging pipe also points to ice.

    A new freeze is not a warranty issue, but it is a warning sign. The line is still too cold. We will look at why it froze again and quote insulation, heat cable or a reroute into a heated space, because patching the same exposed run every winter costs more than fixing the cause once.

    A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm part of the wall. Water drains out of the stem after shutoff. It only works if the hose is disconnected, because a connected hose traps water in the stem where it can freeze and split the valve inside the wall, which you may not notice until spring.

    Keep cold air away from the pipe. Insulation and air sealing do most of the work. Close crawlspace vents for the season, insulate attic and garage runs, swap old hose bibs for frost-free models, and add self-regulating heat cable on any run that still gets too cold despite insulation.

    Shut off the main water valve right away. Turn off power or gas to the water heater next. Move valuables away from the water, open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain the lines, and call us so we can stop the leak, repair the pipe and get water back on.

    Thawing and small pipe repairs usually do not need a permit. Bigger work might. Repiping a section, rerouting a line into a heated wall, or replacing a backflow preventer may require a permit in many jurisdictions, and we pull it when needed so the work is on record.

    You can open the faucet and gently warm an exposed pipe with a hair dryer. Open flame is never safe here. Hidden pipes are better left to us, since a hidden split can flood the wall as it thaws.
