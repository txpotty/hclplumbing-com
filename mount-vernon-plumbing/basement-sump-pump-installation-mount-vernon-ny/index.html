    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Mount Vernon

    Figure on roughly $800 to $2,000 for most basement sump pump installs in Mount Vernon. At the low end you get a pump swap in a pit that is already there, while a new pit cut through the slab, a long discharge run, or a battery backup pushes the total toward the top of that range. We quote after seeing it.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. The secondary, usually battery powered, sits beside it as a backup, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Each pump gets its own check valve.

    Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps cover most of our installs. Horsepower and lift decide which model goes in, then by features like a vertical float or a built-in alarm, so the pump fits your pit, your budget and the amount of water your Mount Vernon basement actually takes on. Cast iron is our default.

    Mostly cast iron pumps from Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty Pumps and Little Giant. Parts for all four are easy to find at supply houses, which matters when a switch fails on a Sunday and you want the pump running again without waiting a week for an order to ship. Common models ride on our trucks.

    A straight pump replacement usually does not need a permit. New pits, new discharge routes and new circuits may, so we look up the local rules and file for a permit when one is needed.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and a concrete patch takes most of a day, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    New York has some of the oldest housing in the country, and many homes still have galvanized steel supply pipes and cast iron drains. Slab homes without a basement rarely need a pump. Houses with basements or below-grade rooms are another story, since they can take on water fast during heavy storms, especially where the ground sheds rain rather than soaking it up. A pit costs less than a cleanup.

    Spring snowmelt and heavy rain can raise water tables quickly in New York, sending water into basements and testing sump pumps. Outages and storms arrive together, so we recommend a battery backup in most basements. Think of it as cheap insurance.
