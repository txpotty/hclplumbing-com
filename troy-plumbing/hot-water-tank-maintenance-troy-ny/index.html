    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. Swapping the rod usually fixes it.     Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Troy

    Street pressure over 80 psi is hard on every fixture in the house, and plumbing codes generally call for a pressure reducing valve above that number. We check static pressure at a hose bib.     Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Troy, NY

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. On gas units we inspect the burner, thermocouple and vent connector. Electric units get a meter test on both elements and a look at the wiring at each thermostat, where loose terminals can scorch and melt the insulation. Both get a flush.

    Rheem and Bradford White Tank Service in Troy

    Rheem, Bradford White, AO Smith and State make most of the tanks in local garages, and each has quirks a tech should know before opening a drain valve.     Electric Thermostat Service in Troy

    Each element on an electric tank has its own thermostat, and the upper one carries a reset button for the high-limit switch. A tripped reset is a symptom. We trace the cause, often a shorted element or a stuck thermostat, before resetting it. Power stays off at the breaker the whole time.

    Standard Tank Flushing in Troy

Then we close the valve and check that it reseals without a drip.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Troy

    Water chemistry decides how fast the anode gets eaten. Softened water is the hardest on rods. A softener can cut rod life to two or three years.

    It is a long rod of magnesium or aluminum screwed into the top of the tank. The rod rusts away in place of the steel. Once it is used up, the water starts attacking the glass lining and the steel behind it, and a tank with a spent anode can rust through in a few years.

    Our techs work on gas and electric tanks from every major maker, plus the store brands built in the same plants. Age of the tank does not matter. We have serviced tanks from the 1990s that still had their original anode, which tells you how rarely most heaters get looked at.

    Three to five years for a magnesium rod is typical on hard water. A softener shortens it further. We measure it every visit so you replace it before the lining rusts.

    About an hour for a typical 40 or 50 gallon tank, sometimes up to two. A badly scaled tank takes longer. A tank that has not been flushed in years can take four or five drain and refill cycles before the hose runs clear, and a seized anode adds another 15 to 30 minutes.

    Private wells serve a large share of rural homes, and they can carry iron, sulfur or hardness that stains fixtures and clogs filters.
