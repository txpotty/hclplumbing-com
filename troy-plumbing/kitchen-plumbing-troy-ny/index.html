    Grease traps are sized by flow, and a three-compartment sink usually needs at least a 20 gallon per minute unit. We set the trap level, install the flow control fitting and vent it so it does not siphon. Cleaning schedules depend on volume. Busy fryer kitchens may need it every few weeks.

    Disposal Replacement in Troy, NY

    Disposals last about eight to twelve years. Water dripping from the bottom of the housing means it is finished.     Burst Supply Lines in Troy, NY

    Supply lines with plastic coupling nuts split more often than braided stainless, especially after years of pressure swings. A failed line under the sink can put gallons per minute into the cabinet and the floor below before anyone notices it. We replace both sides. If one line cracked from age, the other one is about the same age.

    Workstation and Farmhouse Sinks in Troy

    Farmhouse sinks need a cabinet built to carry them, since a 30 inch fireclay bowl can weigh over 100 pounds before water. We check the frame first. Workstation sinks with ledges and accessories often have an offset drain, so the trap and disposal have to be planned around that drain location before the counter crew arrives.

    Grease Trap Repair in Troy, NY

    Rusted inlet tees, cracked lids and missing baffles all let grease pass straight through a trap. We inspect each part during cleaning and replace what has failed, in most cases right then. Without a baffle, a trap is only a box. On big interceptors we schedule the pump-out first and then check the walls, outlet and sampling port.

    InSinkErator Installation in Troy, NY

    Trading an older InSinkErator for an Evolution model is a common request because the newer units are quieter and grind finer. Often the mount stays.     New Disposal Installation in Troy

    Adding a disposal where there never was one means a switched outlet or circuit in the cabinet, and that part is electrical work. We plumb it and can coordinate the wiring. The trap often has to drop a couple of inches because the disposal outlet sits lower than the sink tailpiece did, and the wall stub has to allow it.
