    First-Time Disposal Installation in Troy

    If your kitchen has never had a disposal, we start by checking the sink flange, drain height and electrical supply.     Disposal Rough-In for New Construction in Troy

    At rough-in we set the drain height and the outlet location before the cabinets go in. For a kitchen with a disposal, we usually set the drain stub lower than a plain sink would need, so the trap can sit below the disposal outlet with room for proper fall. A switched outlet goes in at this stage. We mark its spot so it clears the trap.

    Disposal Installation for Double Sinks in Troy, NY

    On a double sink, the disposal goes on one bowl and a continuous waste or end outlet tee on the other. The layout keeps both bowls draining and stops the disposal from pushing water into the second bowl, a problem when the tee is too close to the discharge. We use heavy-wall fittings.

    High-Horsepower Disposal Installation in Troy

    A 3/4 or 1 HP unit takes up more room and weighs more than a basic unit, so we measure the cabinet first. Taller units can push the discharge outlet below the wall stub, and when that happens we either lower the drain at the wall or pick a model with a higher outlet so water does not sit in the chamber. We also check the circuit. Bigger motors draw more at startup.

    Disposal Installation on an Existing Sink in Troy

    With a typical 1/2 or 3/4 HP unit, we remove the old unit if there is one and install the new one on the existing sink. We connect it to the drain, set the P-trap and wire it, then check the clearance under the sink, which can be tight in older Troy homes with shallow cabinets or a water filter mounted on the back wall. Tight cabinets need planning. Sometimes a compact model is the better fit.

    Switching From Batch-Feed to Continuous-Feed in Troy, NY

    Going from batch-feed to continuous-feed means adding a way to switch it on. That can be a wall switch, an air switch button on the sink deck or a switched outlet under the sink, and the choice depends on how hard it is to run wire to the spot you want. An air button skips the wall wiring. The drain usually stays the same.
