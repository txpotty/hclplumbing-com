    Zoeller Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Troy

    New Construction Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Troy, NY

    On a new build, the basin goes in before the slab is poured. With no concrete to cut, it costs the least then, and we stub drains, vent and discharge to the plans for a clean rough-in inspection. Builders like the timing.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Troy

    A high water alarm belongs on every ejector system, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. Audible alarms on their own power or Wi-Fi alarms that text you give warning while the pit is still filling, not after waste reaches the floor. Coastal areas face storm surge and flooding, which can damage water heaters and boilers installed at basement level. Battery backup is an option.

    Emergency Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Troy

    An ejector failure often starts with the high water alarm, followed by slow drains and a smell near the pit. Stop all use of the basement fixtures and call, because once a plumber is on site most emergency replacements take just a few hours. We carry common pumps.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Troy

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps seem similar at first but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.

    Commercial Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Troy, NY

    Businesses in Troy, NY with restrooms, kitchens or break rooms below grade need ejector systems that keep up with heavier, steadier use than a house. A duplex system with an alternating panel is common, so if one pump fails the other keeps things running. Downtime costs money. Each pump can carry the load alone.

    Ejector Pump Installation Cost Comparison in Troy, NY

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin costs the least, often done in a few hours. A new system with a basin, slab excavation, venting through the roof, electrical work and an alarm costs more, but it is the only way to add a bathroom below the sewer line in most homes without a macerating unit. We price both options.

Most problems come from the install, not the hardware, so we take our time on the vent and the lid seal.
