    Light Commercial Sump Pump Replacement in Schenectady, NY

    Light commercial work, think retail basements and small office buildings, usually means a 1/2 HP or larger cast iron pump with a 2-inch discharge and a panel alarm. We match the old pump or step it up. Grinder and ejector pumps are a separate job.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Schenectady

    Basement bathrooms below the sewer line drain into a sealed, vented basin, and the ejector pump in that basin has to pass 2-inch solids. It is a different pump entirely. We replace the ejector and its discharge check valve, reseal the lid gasket, and confirm the vent ties in. Leaky lids let sewer gas into the basement.

    High-Capacity Sump Pump Upgrades in Schenectady

    If your pump runs every 30 seconds during a storm and still loses ground, it is undersized for the water coming in. We time the cycles and measure how fast the pit refills. From there it is math. Often a 1/2 or 3/4 HP pump with more GPM at your lift, plus a 2-inch discharge line, solves it.

    Yard Drainage Pit Pumps in Schenectady, NY

    Yard drainage pits and window well pumps can sit dry from fall to spring and then seize the first time a big storm fills them. We clean the debris out and install a pump built to handle grit. Discharge goes away from the foundation. A bucket test every spring catches problems early.

    Submersible Sump Pump Replacement in Schenectady

    A submersible pump sits at the bottom of the pit, sealed in an oil-filled housing, and it runs quieter than anything else you can put down there. Out comes the old pump, out comes the muck at the bottom of the basin, and the new one goes in on a brick to stay clear of silt. Then a new check valve goes on. Skip that part and the pump short cycles.

    Sump Basin and Pit Replacement in Schenectady, NY

    Old clay tile or 18-inch plastic basins crack and fill with silt, and a pump in a basin that small cycles every few minutes during a heavy rain. The fix is to cut the slab, drop in a bigger poly basin with a bolted lid, and patch the concrete. More volume in the pit means fewer starts per hour. Fewer starts, longer motor life.

    Elevator and Garage Pit Pumps in Schenectady

    Elevator pits and parking garage sumps often require oil-sensing controls that stop the pump from sending hydraulic fluid or motor oil into the storm drain. Pumps and oil-sensing panels get replaced to code. Duplex setups with alternating pumps give you a spare if one quits. Some insurance carriers ask for test records.

    Wayne Sump Pump Replacement in Schenectady

    Wayne makes everything from a thermoplastic quarter-horse pump at the hardware store to cast iron models with vertical switches, and the cheap ones are what we replace most. Most dead Wayne pumps we see have a cooked motor or a float tangled in the power cord. You get a matching Wayne or a sturdier cast iron model, your call. Figure on a couple of hours.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Schenectady

    A standard swap usually runs $500 to $1,200 installed. Add $800 to $1,800 for a battery backup system. Sewage ejector pumps are a bigger job, generally $1,200 to $2,500, because the basin has to be sealed and tied into the plumbing vent system.

    Zoeller, Liberty Pumps, Wayne and Little Giant cover most of what we put in. For a primary pump we usually recommend cast iron. We can usually install a brand you prefer, provided it is rated for your discharge height and basin.

    Pour a five-gallon bucket into the pit every few months and watch the pump start and shut off. Scoop out silt once a year. Also walk outside during a storm and confirm the discharge pipe is actually flowing and not crushed or buried under mulch.

    A battery backup is a second pump, usually 12-volt, that sits slightly above the primary on its own float and runs off a deep-cycle battery. If the main pump dies or the power drops, it takes over. The charger keeps it ready between storms.

    Budget a morning or an afternoon. The work includes pulling the old pump, cleaning the basin, installing the new pump and check valve, and running a fill test. Longer if the basin or discharge needs work.

    The primary pump runs on household current and does the daily work. The backup, battery or water powered, sits a few inches higher and only runs if the primary fails, loses power or cannot keep up with a heavy inflow. It is cheap insurance.

    Seven to ten years is typical. Idle pumps often die early from a stuck switch.
