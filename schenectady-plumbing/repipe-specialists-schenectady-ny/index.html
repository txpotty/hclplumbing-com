    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Schenectady, NY

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. For a typical house, copper often runs 20 to 40 percent above PEX, though some buyers and homeowners like the look and feel of metal pipe and are willing to pay for it. Our quotes for either include permit, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    Copper Repiping in Schenectady, NY

Most copper jobs run a day longer than PEX, since each turn needs its own fitting.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Schenectady

    Most PEX repipes for New York homes cost $5,000 to $12,000, depending on size, bathrooms and access. Copper repipes usually run 20 to 40 percent more.

    Yes, a whole-home repipe in Schenectady, NY usually needs a plumbing permit. We handle it. Our office files the permit, schedules the rough-in inspection while the walls are open and handles the final, so you get a repipe that is on record with the building department.

    Homeowners around Schenectady with hard water often add a softener to protect water heaters and keep mineral buildup out of faucets. Hard water deposits calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe over time. Pressure falls as scale builds. In metal pipe, scale also speeds up corrosion, so repiping with PEX-A and adding a softener or filter during the job can protect the new plumbing and your fixtures for years.

    A documented repipe removes a known risk that buyers and inspectors look for. It helps at sale time. Replacing polybutylene, Kitec or old galvanized makes a home easier to insure and easier to sell, and a permit record plus a transferable warranty gives the next owner proof the work was done.

    We have to open some drywall, but the openings are small and planned. Most are about the size of a sheet of paper. Each cut is a clean square at a fixture or along the route, and we patch and texture all of them so the walls are ready for paint.

    A repipe needs a permit, specialized tools, code knowledge and a passing pressure test. It is not a weekend job. A DIY repipe can leave hidden leaks inside walls, fail inspection or void the pipe manufacturer's warranty, and any of those can cost more than hiring a licensed crew in the first place.

    Fixing one leak at a time works until the leaks start coming every few months. Then the math changes. Each repair means a service call, opened drywall and sometimes an insurance claim, while a repipe replaces all of the aging pipe at once and ends the cycle for decades.

    Both work well, and the best choice depends on your house and your water. PEX costs less. PEX bends, resists scale and handles freezes better, and copper is rigid, UV-tolerant and proven but pricier and vulnerable to aggressive water.
