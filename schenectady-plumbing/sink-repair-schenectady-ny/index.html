    Repair or Replace? Sink Costs in Schenectady, NY

    Repair is cheaper nine times out of ten. When it is close, you get both prices. Some people repair now and remodel next year.

    Hair Clog Clearing in Schenectady

    Hair is the bathroom sink's main problem. We pull the stopper, strip the hair mat off it, then run a small hand auger through the trap and into the wall arm instead of pouring in drain cleaner. The overflow channel gets flushed too. That slot smells when it fills with slime.

    Leaking P-Trap Fix in Schenectady

    Check the slip nuts first. Plastic slip nuts crack when they are overtightened with pliers, and the beveled washers inside them flatten until water seeps out at every joint along the trap. The whole trap gets replaced as a kit. A 1-1/4 inch kit for bath sinks, 1-1/2 inch for kitchens.

    Low Water Pressure at the Sink in Schenectady, NY

    Unscrew the aerator before anything else. A clogged aerator screen causes most low flow at a single faucet, and if cleaning it does not help we check the cartridge and the angle stop for debris. Whole-house low pressure is another call. We test the pressure regulator in that case.

    Pop-Up Stopper Repair in Schenectady, NY

    Stoppers that will not hold water have a linkage problem. Either the clevis slipped on the lift rod or hair has wrapped the stopper so it will not seat. Half the time, a cleaning does it. The other half get a new pop-up drain in a matching finish.

    Cracked or Loose Basin Repair in Schenectady

    Undermount clips let go more often than people think. Failed epoxy lets the bowl sag a little, the caulk splits, and counter water runs right into the cabinet. We reset the bowl on new clips. Hairline cracks in porcelain near the drain mean replacement.

    Sink Parts Replacement in Schenectady, NY

    Most sinks do not need replacing. A new tailpiece or pop-up assembly costs a fraction of a new sink and installs in an hour, and the bowl and faucet you already like stay right where they are. Finishes get matched when the part exists. Discontinued faucets sometimes force a full faucet swap.
