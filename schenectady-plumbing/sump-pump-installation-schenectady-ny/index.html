    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Schenectady, NY

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the pit, so the motor never sits in water. You can reach the motor and the switch without fishing anything out of the pit, which helps in a basement where the pit is shallow or tight. Expect more motor noise than a submersible. We set the float rod and the discharge so the pump cycles cleanly.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Schenectady

    Submersible pumps live at the bottom of the pit with a sealed, oil-cooled motor. They run quieter and move more water. Lids close flat over them. We seal the cord grommet and the lid on every install.

    Combination Sump Pump System Installation in Schenectady, NY

    Two pumps on one riser cover both kinds of failure, a dead motor and a dead outlet. The primary handles daily water. Finished basements are the usual reason. We install them in homes where a flood would ruin flooring and drywall.

    Sump Pump Installation Cost in Schenectady, NY

    Price depends mostly on whether a pit already exists. Swapping a pump in an existing pit is the cheapest job, while a new pit means saw cutting, digging, gravel and a concrete patch, which roughly doubles the labor. Backups add cost. We list each option on a written quote.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Schenectady, NY

    Storms knock out power at the exact moment the pit is filling fastest. The backup pump gets its own float and its own check valve, and it runs off a sealed 12-volt battery when the outlet goes dead. Most run five to seven hours on a full charge. We mount the charger on the wall, off the floor.

    Emergency Sump Pump Replacement in Schenectady, NY

    A dead pump during a storm means the water is already climbing. Our trucks carry 1/3 and 1/2 HP submersibles, check valves and fittings, so in most homes we can pull the old pump and set a new one in one visit. Most swaps take about two hours. We test it with a full pit before leaving.

    High Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring in Schenectady

    A high water alarm is a float switch that sounds when the pit rises past normal. Wi-Fi models send a text too. Rentals need these. We add them to new installs and to pumps already in place.
