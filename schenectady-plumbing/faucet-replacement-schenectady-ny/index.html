    That depends on what is under your sink. New quarter-turn stops and a cooperative old faucet make it a fair DIY job, while corroded stops and fused nuts make it a job for a plumber. Our swaps include a labor warranty.

    Look under the sink first. Stains on the cabinet floor and crust on the connections usually mean the body leaks, and that is a replacement. Check the spout too. A cracked body or a handle that will not close all the way means replace it.
