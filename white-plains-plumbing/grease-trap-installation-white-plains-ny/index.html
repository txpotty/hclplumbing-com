    Commercial Kitchen Grease Trap Installs in White Plains

    Every commercial install starts with a fixture list. The tech walks the kitchen and writes down every fixture that drains to the trap and how fast each one empties. From there we pick a hydromechanical unit or a gravity tank and figure out where it can sit with a straight shot to the building sewer. Fewer bends, fewer clogs.

    Gravity Grease Trap Installation in White Plains

    A gravity interceptor works on time, not machinery. Wastewater slows down in the tank long enough for grease to float and solids to settle. We size by fixture count and retention time, then match the tank to what the local pretreatment office for your building will accept. No moving parts to fail.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in White Plains, NY

    Hydromechanical traps use baffles and a vented flow control to pull grease out of water moving at a set rate, usually 20 to 100 gallons per minute. You can put one under a sink, beside a dish station or in a pit in the slab. The flow control is not optional. Without it, a dumped sink pushes water through faster than the unit is rated for and the grease goes out the outlet. We look for units certified to PDI G-101 or ASME A112.14.3.

    Emergency Grease Trap Services in White Plains, NY

    A grease trap backing up into the floor sinks at the start of Friday dinner service means a closed kitchen and a health inspector who may not let you reopen until it is fixed. We send a tech with a jetter and a camera to find out whether the trap or the line on either side is the problem. Often it is the outlet. A failed trap can often get a stopgap unit that night and a proper replacement later in the week.

    Grease Trap Installation for Schools and Institutions in White Plains

    School cafeterias and hospital kitchens cook in big batches and wash in big batches, which means large pot sinks and a dish machine running hard for two hours after each meal. An outdoor gravity tank is the usual answer for that volume. We schedule around the calendar. School jobs fit into breaks, and hospital jobs get phased so meals keep going out.

    Under-Sink Grease Trap Setup in White Plains, NY

    Small cafes and coffee bars often get by with a 10 to 25 GPM hydromechanical trap tucked under the prep sink or beside it. The unit goes on the floor or in a shallow recess, and the flow control fitting goes upstream. Clearance matters most. We leave room to pull the lid and baffle without unhooking the sink, because a trap nobody can open is a trap nobody cleans.

    Grease Trap Sizing and Placement in White Plains, NY

    Sizing starts with fixture drain rates and ends with where the trap can physically go. A trap 40 feet from the sink lets grease cool and stick in the pipe before it ever reaches the unit. Closer is better. We also check that a pump truck can reach an outdoor tank with about 100 feet of hose, since a tank the hauler cannot reach costs you more on every service.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in White Plains

    Installed prices for small indoor hydromechanical traps typically fall between $1,500 and $5,000. Outdoor tanks cost more, mostly for digging and putting the site back. Brand is a small part of it. The written estimate holds unless the dig turns up a buried surprise.

    Indoor hydromechanical traps and outdoor gravity interceptors cover nearly every job we do. Automatic grease removal units are a pricier option for kitchens that fry all day. Most cafes go hydromechanical. High-volume kitchens usually get an outdoor gravity tank, especially if the sewer authority asks for one.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. Busy kitchens with small indoor traps often hit that mark every week. Outdoor tanks go longer. Many gravity interceptors are pumped every 30 to 90 days, and your local FOG program may set a maximum interval no matter what the trap looks like.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. You get copies of every approval for your files. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    Pump-outs every couple of weeks on a trap that used to go two months is the clearest sign you have outgrown it or the baffles have failed. Grease odor in the dining room is another. Rust shows up too. On steel traps we look for pinholes at the seams, and on any trap we check whether the outlet line downstream is coated with grease that should have been caught.

    Two warranties apply here. The trap body carries the maker's coverage, which runs from one year on some steel units to much longer on plastic. Our labor warranty covers the joints and the setting, and you get it in writing up front.

    Half a day for most indoor units. We set it, connect it, add the flow control and test it with water. Gravity interceptors take two to five days on site, plus time for the inspector to see the tank before backfill and again at final. Permits add lead time.

    Coverage comes from two places. Your trap carries a factory warranty that varies by brand and material. We put our labor warranty in writing on the invoice, covering leaks at the joints we made and any problem with how we set and leveled the unit. Damage from skipped cleanings falls outside it.
