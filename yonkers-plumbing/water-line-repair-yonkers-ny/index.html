    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Yonkers

    A spot repair on an accessible service line typically runs $350 to $1,500 depending on depth and access. Full replacement from meter to house usually costs $1,800 to $5,500, and trenchless replacement often falls between $3,000 and $7,000. We give you a written price before starting.

    The duration of a water line repair depends on the severity and type of repair. A simple spot repair might take a few hours, while a more extensive replacement, especially if it involves excavation or trenchless methods, could take one to three days. We always work to complete the work efficiently while maintaining solid quality.

    In most communities the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. The homeowner is in charge of the service line from the meter to the house. We confirm which side the leak is on before quoting you.

    Common signs include a wet or unusually green patch of yard, a hissing sound near the meter, low pressure at every fixture and a water bill that climbs without explanation. Dirty or rusty water can also signal a damaged line. If your meter moves with all fixtures off, you probably have a leak.

    We typically install Type K copper, PEX rated for direct burial or HDPE for trenchless pulls. Each holds up well in most soils when properly bedded. We will recommend the right material based on your soil, line length and price range.

    Most spot repairs are finished in two to four hours. A full replacement usually takes one day, and trenchless jobs are often done in a day as well. Water is usually back on the same day.

    Common signs of a water line leak include an unexplained increase in your water bill, damp or soggy spots in your yard (even during dry weather), a clear drop in water pressure, discolored water, or the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use. If you notice any of these, it's wise to call a professional plumber right away.

    A homeowner can sometimes tighten a leaking fitting at an exposed hose bib. Buried service line repairs require locating, safe excavation, 811 utility marking and code-approved fittings. Mistakes can cause bigger leaks, so most homeowners hire a licensed plumber.
